# Data Governance Principles

## Introduction

This document outlines the data governance principles for the CPI Hub platform. These principles guide how data is collected, stored, processed, and shared throughout the system. Our goal is to ensure data is managed securely, ethically, and in compliance with relevant regulations while maximizing its value to stakeholders.

## Core Principles

### 1. Data Ownership and Accountability

- **Clear Ownership**: Every data asset has a clearly defined owner responsible for its accuracy, quality, and appropriate use.
- **Accountability Framework**: Stakeholders are accountable for their data-related actions according to defined roles and responsibilities.
- **Data Stewardship**: Data stewards are appointed to oversee data quality, metadata management, and policy compliance.

### 2. Data Classification and Handling

- **Classification Framework**: All data is classified according to sensitivity, criticality, and regulatory requirements:
  - **Level 1 (Public)**: Information that can be freely disclosed.
  - **Level 2 (Internal)**: Information for internal use that would not cause material harm if disclosed.
  - **Level 3 (Confidential)**: Sensitive information that requires protection and could cause harm if disclosed.
  - **Level 4 (Restricted)**: Highly sensitive information subject to regulatory requirements or that could cause significant harm if disclosed.

- **Handling Guidelines**: Each classification level has specific handling, storage, transmission, and disposal requirements.

### 3. Data Quality and Integrity

- **Quality Standards**: All data must meet defined quality standards for accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, and relevancy.
- **Data Validation**: Automated and manual validation processes ensure data meets quality standards at all stages of its lifecycle.
- **Quality Monitoring**: Continuous monitoring processes track and measure data quality.
- **Remediation Processes**: Clear procedures for addressing data quality issues when identified.

### 4. Data Security and Privacy

- **Privacy by Design**: Privacy considerations are built into all data processes from the beginning.
- **Need-to-Know Access**: Access to data is granted only to those who need it for legitimate business purposes.
- **Data Minimization**: Only necessary data is collected and retained only as long as required.
- **Encryption Standards**: Data is encrypted both in transit and at rest according to classification levels.
- **Anonymization**: Personal data is anonymized or pseudonymized whenever possible.

### 5. Data Lifecycle Management

- **Data Creation**: Standardized processes for data creation ensure consistency and quality.
- **Data Storage**: Appropriate storage solutions based on data classification, access patterns, and retention requirements.
- **Data Retention**: Clear policies define how long data should be retained based on business needs and regulatory requirements.
- **Data Archiving**: Structured processes for moving inactive data to cost-effective, secure archive storage.
- **Data Disposal**: Secure deletion processes ensure data is properly destroyed when no longer needed.

### 6. Metadata Management

- **Metadata Standards**: Consistent metadata standards describing data elements, lineage, quality, and usage.
- **Data Dictionary**: Centralized repository documenting all data definitions, attributes, and relationships.
- **Search and Discovery**: Metadata facilitates data discovery and understanding for authorized users.

### 7. Regulatory Compliance and Ethics

- **Compliance Framework**: Processes to ensure adherence to relevant regulations (GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, etc.).
- **Data Ethics**: Ethical considerations guide all data-related decisions, particularly for AI and analytics.
- **Bias Prevention**: Processes to identify and mitigate bias in data collection, processing, and analysis.
- **Transparency**: Clear communication about how data is collected, used, and shared.

### 8. Data Integration and Interoperability

- **Integration Standards**: Standardized approaches for data movement and transformation.
- **API Governance**: Management of APIs for data access with appropriate security and performance controls.
- **Master Data Management**: Consistent management of critical shared data entities.
- **Data Modeling Standards**: Structured approach to data modeling ensuring consistency across the organization.

### 9. AI and Analytics Governance

- **AI Ethics Framework**: Guidelines for ethical use of AI and machine learning with data.
- **Algorithm Transparency**: Documentation of algorithms and models for traceability and explainability.
- **Training Data Governance**: Processes to ensure training data is representative, unbiased, and properly sourced.
- **Model Validation**: Standards for validating model accuracy, fairness, and appropriateness.

### 10. Data Access and Sharing

- **Access Control**: Role-based access controls aligned with data classification levels.
- **Sharing Agreements**: Formal agreements defining terms for external data sharing.
- **Third-Party Data Management**: Standards for acquiring, validating, and managing third-party data.
- **Open Data Principles**: Framework for determining when and how to share data openly.

## Governance Structure

### Roles and Responsibilities

- **Data Governance Board**: Strategic oversight of data governance activities.
- **Chief Data Officer**: Executive responsible for data strategy and governance.
- **Data Stewards**: Subject matter experts responsible for data quality in their domains.
- **Data Custodians**: IT staff responsible for technical implementation of data controls.
- **Data Users**: All employees who create, access, or manipulate data.

### Processes

- **Policy Management**: Development, approval, and maintenance of data policies.
- **Issue Resolution**: Process for addressing data-related issues and conflicts.
- **Change Management**: Controlled approach to data-related changes.
- **Monitoring and Reporting**: Regular assessment and reporting on governance effectiveness.

## Implementation

### Phased Approach

1. **Foundation Phase**: Establish governance structure, define critical policies.
2. **Implementation Phase**: Roll out policies and procedures, conduct training.
3. **Maturity Phase**: Continuous improvement, metrics-driven governance.

### Tools and Technology

- **Metadata Repository**: Central storage of data definitions and lineage.
- **Data Quality Tools**: Automated monitoring of data quality metrics.
- **Access Control Systems**: Technical enforcement of access policies.
- **Data Lineage Tools**: Tracking of data movement and transformations.

### Training and Awareness

- **Governance Training**: Education on policies, roles, and responsibilities.
- **Data Literacy Program**: Building data skills across the organization.
- **Communication Plan**: Regular updates on governance activities and changes.

## Compliance Monitoring and Reporting

- **Governance Metrics**: Key performance indicators for measuring governance effectiveness.
- **Compliance Audits**: Regular audits of governance policy compliance.
- **Executive Reporting**: Regular updates to leadership on governance status.

## Continuous Improvement

- **Policy Review Cycle**: Regular review and updates to governance policies.
- **Feedback Mechanisms**: Channels for stakeholders to provide governance feedback.
- **Maturity Assessments**: Periodic assessment of governance maturity.

## Conclusion

These data governance principles provide a framework for managing the CPI Hub's data assets as valuable organizational resources. By following these principles, we ensure data is accurate, secure, compliant, and maximally valuable to the organization and its stakeholders.
